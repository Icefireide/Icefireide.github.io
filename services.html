Process

How I Work

Most problems on a broadcast are decided days before anyone goes live. This is how I keep show day boring.

01
The build is finished two days out

Scenes, music, transitions and graphics are complete and frozen 48 hours before doors. That isn’t a target, it’s a deadline — it means rehearsal exists to find problems while there’s still time to fix them, rather than being the first time anyone sees the show.

02
The show runs off a live control sheet

Graphics are driven from a Google Sheet wired into the broadcast. Team names, scores, rosters, lower thirds — change a cell and it changes on air. Nothing gets re-exported, nothing gets rebuilt mid-show, and anyone on the crew with the link can update the broadcast without touching the production machine.

03
Scope is agreed in writing first

A contractor agreement and a statement of work before anything starts — deliverables, timeline and price settled up front. Anything added later goes through a change order, so nobody discovers a disagreement on invoice day.

04
Documented, not dependent on me

Tooling ships with an operator manual, and the control sheet means the broadcast is driven from something your team already knows how to use. If they need to run it without me — a scheduling clash, a second stage, next season — they can. I’d rather be rebooked than be a single point of failure.
